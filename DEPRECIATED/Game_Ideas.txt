Bullshit 
    Best for 4 to 10 players. A classic card game where players discard cards face-down in 
    ascending numerical order, openly lying about what they are playing. If someone calls "Bullshit!" 
    and catches a liar, the liar drinks and picks up the deck; if the accuser is wrong, they
    drink instead.


fuck the dealer 
    Best for 4 to 8 players. One player acts as the dealer. Moving around the circle, players guess 
    the value of the dealer's hidden card. If they guess wrong twice, they drink the difference; if 
    they guess right, the dealer drinks heavily.


ride the bus 
    Great for small-to-medium groups. It features multiple phases where players guess attributes of cards 
    (e.g., Red or Black? Higher or Lower?) before a final, high-stakes "bus ride" elimination round.


quaters 
    Best for 3 to 6 players. Players take turns trying to bounce a physical coin (traditionally a quarter) 
    off the table and directly into a shot glass or a chalice cup filled with a drink in the center.

drunk jenga 
     Write dares, rules, or drink commands on the side of Jenga blocks before building the tower. Whenever 
     a player pulls a block out, they must perform the written action. If the tower falls over, that player 
     has to chug.


tower roulette 
    An original twist on Drunk Jenga using a standard block tower. Before starting, pull 5 random blocks and 
    paint their ends red. When a player successfully pulls a red block, they become the "Dictator" and can hand 
    out drinks or invent a temporary house rule. If they crash the tower while pulling a red block, they have to 
    finish two full drinks.


medusa 
     Best for 5 to 10 players. Everyone starts with their heads down on the table. On the count of three, everyone 
     looks up and stares directly at another player. If you are looking at someone who is not looking at you, you 
     are safe. If you make direct eye contact with someone looking back at you, both players must yell "Medusa!" 
     and the person that says it last must finish a drink.


name game 
    Best for 3 to 8 players. Ideal for developer groups. The first player says a tech, gaming, or pop-culture entity 
    (e.g., "JavaScript"). The next player must say an entity that starts with the last letter of the previous 
    word (e.g., "Tomcat"). If a player says an acronym (e.g., "AWS"), the direction of the circle reverses. 
    Pausing for more than 3 seconds or repeating a word requires a drink.
